The bottom line

Three findings that matter

What a technology employer should take away from the analysis, before considering policy changes.

FINDING 01 / WORK ARRANGEMENT

Remote workers report higher pay.

The remote group's average annual salary exceeds the hybrid group's by $15.4k. Both the statistical test and the 95% confidence intervals support a difference in these survey responses.

FINDING 02 / EDUCATION

Education correlates with earnings.

Among US and Canadian respondents, higher degrees are associated with higher average salaries. However, education group accounts for less than 1% of observed salary variation.

FINDING 03 / INTERPRETATION

Association is not causation.

Pay varies considerably within each group. Differences in job function, geography, seniority, and respondent selection may contribute; these results alone should not dictate hiring or work-mode policy.

04 / Technical notes

How to interpret these results

Transparent methodology, sampling rules, and limits for decision-makers. All figures on this page are from the supplied report, with no respondent-level records included.

Data & collection

2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey, a voluntary observational survey. Its cleaned assignment dataset excludes responses with missing annual compensation. These respondents are not a random census of all developers.

Exploratory figures

Excluded one invalid job-satisfaction response of 60, and ages below 18 or above 100 from the age analysis. Salary-by-title extremes remain in the exploratory chart, so some group means are outlier-sensitive.

Hybrid vs remote

Started with 14,737 eligible respondents. Removed 74 high salaries (32 hybrid, 42 remote) above the combined 99.5th percentile ($606,400), retaining 14,663. Lower salaries were retained to avoid arbitrarily excluding possible valid cases.

Education analysis

Restricted to US and Canadian respondents with Bachelor, Master or Professional degrees. Removed 22 observations above the combined 99.5th percentile ($882,585), retaining 4,341 responses.

Inferential methods

Two-sided pooled and Welch t-tests, Levene's tests, Mood's median test, classical and Welch ANOVA, bootstrap simulations (10,000 repetitions), and 95% confidence intervals. The bootstrap keeps each group's original sample size.

Policy limitations

Role, experience, country, seniority, self-selection and pay-reporting variation may confound observed associations. Statistical significance is not the same as a large practical effect or a causal relationship.
